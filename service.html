What Is a Dharma Service?

Meeting in the Dharma

A Dharma service is a gathering that usually consists of religious ritual and activities such as Buddhist chanting, Dharma talks, and offerings.

These gatherings aim to deepen practitioners’ connection to Buddhist teachings and to receive blessings from Buddhas and Bodhisattvas through sincere chanting and prayer. The Dharma talk is essential to each gathering — the goal is to enhance every devotee’s confidence and joy in the Buddha Dharma.

Sunday mornings are the best time to attain the joy of the Dharma — creating fellowship and taking the Dharma as teacher, path, and source of joy.
Two Kinds of Service

Regular & Annual

Regular Dharma Services

Held weekly on Sunday mornings, designed to be accessible for modern practitioners seeking a consistent rhythm of practice.

Annual Dharma Services

Special observances held throughout the year, marking significant dates in the Buddhist and lunar calendars.

Spiritual Care

Care Beyond the Temple Walls

Buddha’s Light Spiritual Care

Founded in late 2020, the temple’s Spiritual Care Group offers compassionate, non-denominational support for anyone facing life’s harder passages — regardless of faith or cultural background.

A team of trained counsellors, coordinators, and volunteers provides one-on-one spiritual and psychotherapeutic counselling, support for stress, anxiety, and depression, and guidance through grief, loss, and end-of-life transitions. The group also runs peer support programs and visits residents in long-term care homes, hospitals, hospices, and correctional facilities, extending care to people who may not be able to come to the temple themselves.

All services are offered free of charge, in the spirit of Humanistic Buddhism — donations to support the program are always welcome.
How the Team Helps

Four Ways We Show Up

One-on-One Counselling

Individual spiritual and psychotherapeutic support for stress, anxiety, depression, grief, and loss.

End-of-Life & Palliative Support

Compassionate accompaniment through life’s final chapter, for patients and their families alike.

Outreach Visits

Regular visits to long-term care homes, hospitals, hospices, and correctional facilities.

Peer Support

Group programs that let people facing similar challenges support and accompany one another.
